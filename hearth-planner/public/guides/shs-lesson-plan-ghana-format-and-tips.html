SHS Lesson Plan in Ghana: Format, Tips and Free Plans

A Senior High School lesson plan has the same bones as a JHS or primary plan, but SHS classes are bigger, lessons are often longer, and learners are preparing for WASSCE. That changes how you plan the main part of the lesson and how you check learning. This guide covers the format, the differences from lower classes, and a few habits that make SHS plans quicker to write and easier to teach.

The parts of an SHS lesson plan

Use the same headings as any GES plan, and keep them short.

  • Details: date, class, subject, programme or group, number of learners, and the period or double period.
  • Topic, with the strand or unit from your subject's curriculum or syllabus.
  • Content standard and learning indicator, copied as published.
  • Lesson objective in your own words, using verbs you can observe.
  • Teaching and learning materials.
  • Phases: starter, main instruction and closure, each with teacher and learner activities and time.
  • Assessment: the questions or task you will use, with a few WASSCE-style items where they fit.

What is different at SHS

Learners can read longer texts, work with more abstract ideas, and handle more independent practice. Use that. A plan that only has the teacher talking wastes the time, so build in problem solving, short discussions, and learners explaining answers to each other.

Planning a double period

A double period can feel long. Split it into two blocks. Teach and model in the first block, then give learners a harder task in the second, such as a past question or a short case study. Finish with five minutes of reflection so you know who needs help next time.

Connect each lesson to the examination

Look at how your topic appears in past WASSCE questions. Add one or two similar questions to the closure, and tell learners how marks are earned: showing working, giving a reason, or using the right term. Do this lightly. The lesson is still about understanding, not only about passing.

Large classes and mixed ability

With 50 or more learners, group work needs structure. Give each group a role (reader, recorder, speaker) and one clear task. Write a quick extension question for groups that finish early and an easier starting step for those who struggle. Put both in your plan, so you are not inventing them in the middle of the lesson.

In short

An SHS lesson plan is a normal GES plan with more learner thinking, a plan for double periods, and a link to WASSCE-style questions. Keep it short, include extension and support, and check it against your syllabus.
