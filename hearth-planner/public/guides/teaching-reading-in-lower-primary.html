Teaching Early Reading in Lower Primary: Sounds, Words and Stories

Reading is the key that opens every other subject. In Basic 1 to 3, learners move from recognising sounds to reading sentences and short stories. Children learn at different speeds, so your job is to give regular, short, well-structured practice. Here is a simple sequence you can follow.

Start with listening and speaking

Children read what they can already say. Use songs, rhymes and stories in the language that learners use best. Many Ghanaian lower primary classes teach first in a Ghanaian language, and this is a strength. Skills of listening for sounds transfer across languages.

Teach letter sounds, not only letter names

Introduce a few sounds at a time, such as s, a, t, p, i, n. Link each to an action and a picture. As soon as learners know a handful of sounds, let them blend them into simple words such as sat, pin, tap.

Blend and segment

  • Blending: say the sounds, then the word. "s-a-t, sat."
  • Segmenting: say the word, then tap out the sounds. "Sat: s-a-t."
  • Practise both daily for five minutes. They are the foundation of reading and spelling.

Build a bank of common words

Some words appear everywhere and are not easy to sound out, such as the, was, said and you. Teach a few at a time with flashcards and use them in simple sentences so that learners see them in context.

Read aloud every day

Read a short story to the class with expression and ask a question or two. Learners learn how stories work, pick up new words and enjoy books. Choose stories linked to local life, such as markets, farms and festivals.

Let learners read, often and in small groups

  • Pair reading: partners take turns reading a sentence.
  • Group reading: a small group reads together while you listen to each child.
  • Take-home cards: simple word or sentence cards for practice with a family member.

Check progress simply

Every few weeks, listen to each child read a short list of letters and words. Note which sounds they know and which need more practice. Group learners by need for a few minutes of focused help, then change the groups.

Involve parents

Ask parents to listen to their child read for ten minutes a day, in any language they share. You are not asking them to teach, only to listen and encourage.

In short

Teach sounds, blend them into words, read aloud daily, and give every child regular turns to read. Short, frequent practice beats long, occasional lessons.
