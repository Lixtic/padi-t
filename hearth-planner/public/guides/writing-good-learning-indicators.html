Writing Learning Indicators and Objectives That You Can Actually Measure

The learning indicator is the heart of a lesson plan. If it is vague, the whole lesson drifts. If it is clear, the activities and the check at the end almost write themselves. Here is how to write indicators and objectives that work.

Indicator and objective: what is the difference?

The indicator comes from the curriculum and describes what a learner should show at the end of a stretch of teaching. Your lesson objective is how that looks in today's lesson, in your words, for your class. They should point in the same direction.

Use a verb you can see

Replace "understand" and "know" with an action that produces evidence. Learners can name, list, match, sort, draw, measure, calculate, retell, compare, explain in their own words, or give an example.

  • Weak: Learners will understand fractions.
  • Better: Learners will be able to shade one half and one quarter of a shape and explain which part is larger.
  • Weak: Learners will know the parts of a plant.
  • Better: Learners will be able to label the root, stem, leaf and flower on a drawing and state one job of each.

Make it small enough for one lesson

If your objective needs three different activities and two kinds of marking, it is probably two objectives. Split it. A realistic lesson teaches one main idea well. Learners remember more when you slow down.

Add the condition and the standard when it helps

Sometimes it is useful to say how well or under what condition. "Using bottle tops, learners will be able to group numbers up to 20 in tens and ones, getting at least four of five examples right." This gives you a clear success mark for your exit task.

Test your objective with two questions

If the answer to either is no, rewrite it before you plan the activities.

  • Could I tell, by looking at a learner's work, whether they reached it?
  • Does it fit inside the time I have, with practice and not only explanation?

Match the objective to your check

Your closing question or task should test exactly what the objective says. If the objective is to compare two numbers, the exit question asks them to compare, not to recite a rule. Aligning the two is the quickest way to improve the quality of your teaching.

In short

Choose an observable verb, keep the objective small, and make sure the closing task tests the same thing. A clear objective makes the rest of the plan easier.
