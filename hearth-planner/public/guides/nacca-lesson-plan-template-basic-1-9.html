NaCCA Lesson Plan Template, Basic 1 to 9 (Free Example)

Teachers in Ghana often ask for "the NaCCA lesson plan template". NaCCA publishes the curriculum, with its strands, content standards and learning indicators. Schools and circuits then ask for a written plan that follows it, and the layout can differ a little from one school to the next. This guide gives you a clean template that covers what most head teachers and circuit supervisors look for, from Basic 1 to Basic 9, and shows how to fill it in.

The headings to put at the top

Start with a small table of details. It tells a reader at a glance which part of the curriculum you are teaching.

  • Date, week and lesson length (for example 60 minutes).
  • Class, subject and number of learners.
  • Strand and sub-strand, copied from the scheme of learning.
  • Content standard and learning indicator, copied exactly as published in the curriculum.
  • Core competencies you expect to build, such as critical thinking or communication.
  • Teaching and learning materials (TLMs).
  • Reference, for example the curriculum page and the textbook page.

The body of the plan: three phases

Most plans use a table with a column for the phase, a column for what the teacher does, a column for what learners do, and a column for the time. Use three phases.

  • Starter (about 10 minutes): get attention and link to what learners already know. A question, a song, a picture or a quick recap game works well.
  • Main instruction (about 35 to 40 minutes): teach the new idea, model it, then let learners practise in pairs or groups. This is where most of the learning happens.
  • Closure and reflection (about 10 minutes): ask learners to show what they learned, tie it back to the objective, and give a short task or exit question.

Write an objective you can see

Put one lesson objective under the indicator, in your own words: "By the end of the lesson, learners will be able to name three parts of a plant and say what each does." Use verbs you can observe, such as name, sort, measure, explain or draw. Keep the official indicator as it is, and write your objective separately, so nobody mistakes your wording for the curriculum text.

A short example

Here is the shape of a filled-in plan for Basic 4 Science. Treat it as a pattern, and check the exact strand and indicator in your own curriculum.

  • Details: Basic 4, Science, 40 learners, 60 minutes, Week 3.
  • Objective: learners will be able to sort materials into those that float and those that sink.
  • TLMs: a basin of water, a stone, a cork, a leaf, a coin and a plastic lid.
  • Starter: ask what happens when we drop a stone into a river. Let three learners answer.
  • Main: show each item, ask learners to predict, test it, and record in a two-column table. Groups repeat with their own objects.
  • Closure: each group names one item that surprised them. Exit question: why does a heavy ship float?

How to check your plan before you use it

Read it once from the learner side. Is there something for learners to do in every phase, not only things for you to say? Is the time realistic? Can you really get every resource listed? Does the closure test the same thing the objective promised? If you can answer yes to all four, the plan is ready.

In short

A good template is simple: details at the top, an official indicator, your own objective, three phases with learner activities, and a quick check at the end. Fill it in from the scheme of learning and keep it short enough to use.
