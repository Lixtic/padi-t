Teaching a Large Class: Practical Strategies That Work With Limited Resources

Many Ghanaian teachers handle classes of fifty, sixty or more learners, sometimes with few textbooks and crowded rooms. You cannot give each child one-to-one time, but you can make large classes work with a few routines. These strategies come from common classroom practice and are meant to be adapted to your own room.

Set up routines in the first weeks

Large classes run on routines. Teach learners how to get materials, how to move into groups, how to ask for help and what a quiet signal means. Practise each until it takes under a minute. Time spent here is returned many times over.

Use groups with clear roles

Groups of four to six let more learners speak and share scarce books. Give each group member a job: reader, writer, timekeeper, reporter. Rotate the jobs weekly so that everyone practises. Keep the task simple and the time limited, so groups do not drift.

Use peer teaching and helpers

  • Pair a confident learner with a learner who is struggling, and change pairs often.
  • Let early finishers check a neighbour's work against an example.
  • Train two or three learners to hand out and collect materials.

Make the board work hard

If textbooks are few, put the key question, the example and the task on the board before the lesson. Write clearly and large, and use columns so that learners can copy in order. A well-organised board is a shared textbook.

Check understanding with the whole class

  • Ask all learners to answer on a slate, in an exercise book or with a hand signal.
  • Use choral response for simple recall, then follow with an individual explanation.
  • Sample a few books each day rather than marking all of them. Over a week you will have seen everyone.

Mark smart

Marking sixty books every night is not sustainable. Mix teacher marking, peer marking and self-marking with a marking guide on the board. Spot-check the peer marking. Keep your own detailed marking for the tasks that count towards a grade.

Plan for movement and noise

Arrange seats so that you can walk through the room. Use a clear signal to bring attention back, such as a raised hand or a clap pattern. Keep the pace brisk, because idle time is when noise grows.

In short

Routines, group roles, peer help and smart marking make a big class manageable. Aim for every learner to do something in every lesson, even when you cannot reach each one personally.
