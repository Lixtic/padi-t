A Simple BECE Revision Timetable for JHS 3 Students

A timetable turns good intentions into action. The best one is simple enough to follow and flexible enough to adjust. This guide shows how to build one for JHS 3, with a sample week you can copy and change.

Step 1: List your subjects and weak points

Write each subject and, beneath it, the topics you find hardest. Ask your teachers if you are not sure. Mark the weakest topics with a star. These get extra time.

Step 2: Count the weeks you have

Count the weeks to the exam and divide them into three stages. In the first stage, revise topics from the beginning of JHS. In the second, do mixed practice and past questions. In the last few weeks, practise full papers and fix the remaining gaps.

Step 3: Fit sessions into your real life

Be honest about school hours, chores, church or mosque, and travel. Fit in sessions of 30 to 45 minutes with a short break between them. A realistic timetable you keep is better than an ambitious one you abandon.

A sample weekday after school

  • 4:30 pm to 5:15 pm: Mathematics, one topic and some practice questions.
  • 5:15 pm to 5:30 pm: Break, water and a snack.
  • 5:30 pm to 6:15 pm: English Language, grammar or comprehension practice.
  • After supper: 30 minutes of a lighter subject or reading through the day's notes.

A sample weekend

  • Saturday morning: a full past paper in one subject, timed.
  • Saturday afternoon: mark it and correct the mistakes.
  • Sunday: lighter review, rest and preparation for the week.

Study actively, not just by reading

  • Answer questions without looking at notes, then check.
  • Explain a topic aloud to a sibling or friend.
  • Make short summary cards for key terms and formulas.
  • Rewrite a wrong answer correctly in a "mistakes book" and read it weekly.

Balance subjects

Mix a harder subject with an easier one in the same evening. Do not leave any subject untouched for more than a week. Give extra time to subjects that carry heavy weight in the results, but do not neglect the others.

Review and adjust weekly

At the end of each week, ask what went well and what did not. Move topics around if needed. Tick off what you completed, because seeing progress builds confidence.

In short

List weak topics, divide the weeks into stages, keep sessions short, practise actively and review the plan each week.
