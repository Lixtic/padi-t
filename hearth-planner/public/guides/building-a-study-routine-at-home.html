Building a Study Routine at Home That Children Will Actually Follow

Children learn best when study is a normal part of the day, not a battle. A good routine is short, predictable and kind. These ideas work for primary and JHS children, and you can adjust them to your family's schedule.

Decide the time together

Ask your child when they feel most awake, and fit a daily session around that and your family's routine. Children who help choose the time are more likely to keep to it.

Match the length to the age

  • Basic 1 to 3: about 15 to 20 minutes, with reading aloud and a short task.
  • Basic 4 to 6: about 30 to 40 minutes.
  • JHS: about 45 to 90 minutes, split into two sessions with a break.

Set up the space

Choose a place with enough light and a flat surface. Keep pencils, a ruler, notebooks and a water bottle nearby, so that your child does not wander off to find them.

Begin with something easy

Start with a task your child can do well, which builds momentum. Then do the harder work, and finish with a short review of what was learned.

Use short breaks

After about 25 to 30 minutes, take a five-minute break to stretch and drink water. A short break makes the next session more productive.

Limit distractions

  • Put phones in another room during study time.
  • Switch off the television.
  • Agree on a signal for "please do not disturb" when others are in the house.

Keep it positive

Praise effort and progress. Mark off completed days on a simple chart. A small, healthy reward at the end of a good week, such as a family game or a favourite meal, helps. Avoid punishing children with extra study, which makes them dislike it.

Include reading

Whatever else is on the timetable, ask your child to read for at least ten minutes a day. Reading builds vocabulary and comprehension, which help in every subject.

Be flexible

Some days will go badly. If your child is tired or unwell, shorten the session or skip it, and return to normal the next day. The goal is a habit that lasts all year.

In short

Short, regular sessions at a time your child helps to choose, in a calm place, with praise and breaks, create a habit that lasts.
