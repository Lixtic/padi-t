GES Teaching Career Grades and School Positions Explained

The GES Scheme of Service separates grade structures from positional appointments. This distinction matters: a grade describes a staff member’s place in the service structure, while a position describes a role they may be appointed to perform. The teaching-class tables in Part II show how these are connected.

Professional grades and teaching-class positions

The teaching-class table maps Principal Superintendent (PS) to Teacher (Basic School) and Teacher (SHS), as well as schedule officer/teacher roles. Assistant Director II is mapped to positions including Housemaster and Assistant Headteacher of KG/JHS/Primary. Assistant Director I is mapped to School Improvement and Support Officer (SISO), Assistant Head of SHS, Senior Housemaster/Mistress, Head of Department (SHS), Headteacher of KG/JHS/Primary, and schedule officer/teacher (printed page 535).

At higher levels, Deputy Director is mapped to roles such as Deputy Unit Head at Headquarters, frontline officer at regional or district level, Head of SHS and schedule officer/teacher. Director II and Director I include district, regional and headquarters leadership roles, subject to the specific position and appointment requirements in the Scheme (printed pages 535–536).

Sub-professional teaching-class grades

The table also lists Senior Superintendent I (SS I), Senior Superintendent II (SS II) and Assistant Superintendent (AS), each with a corresponding schedule officer/teacher designation (printed page 536). The Scheme’s grade-composition and positional-appointment sections should be read together when interpreting these classifications.

Positions marked with an asterisk

The teaching-position table marks certain appointments with an asterisk. The accompanying note says these appointments are tenured for a standard four-year term, renewable once based on performance. At the end of the tenure, an officer may apply for renewal through appraisal and interview, or be retained at the grade level without holding the position. After two four-year terms, the note specifies a four-year cooling-off period before reapplying for the same position, subject to the stated conditions. Officers may apply for other positions for which they qualify when vacancies arise (printed page 536).

Do not assume every role is tenured: check whether the specific position carries the asterisk and read the appointment note in full.

What this means for a classroom teacher

  • Do not assume that a position title automatically changes your substantive grade.
  • When considering a school leadership role, check both the grade mapping and the position-specific qualifications and appointment process.
  • Keep evidence of teaching practice, leadership responsibilities, professional learning and performance in a form that can be verified.
  • Ask your district or regional HR office to clarify how the Scheme applies to your current appointment and career path.

Where to look in the document

Use the teaching-position overview on printed pages 533–536 first. Then locate the relevant detailed grade profile in Part I and any position-specific requirements in Part II. The contents page is the quickest way to find the relevant division, unit or position.

In short

A grade and a school position are connected but not identical. Check the teaching-class mapping, the specific appointment conditions and the detailed grade profile before drawing conclusions about a role.
