Helping Your Child With Homework When You Are Busy or Did Not Go Far in School

Many parents worry that they cannot help with homework because they work long hours, because the subjects have changed, or because they did not have the chance to go far in school themselves. You do not need to be an expert. Children do better when someone shows interest, sets a routine and asks good questions. Here is how.

Make a time and a place

Choose a regular time, perhaps after the evening meal or after chores, and a place with light, a flat surface and little noise. If the house is crowded, even one corner kept for homework helps. Switch off the television and put phones away during this time.

Start by asking, not by telling

When a child explains something to you, they learn it better. You do not need to know the answer to ask the question.

  • "What did you learn today?"
  • "What is the homework, and which part is hardest?"
  • "Can you show me how you did the first one?"

Help them get started, not finished

If your child is stuck, read the question together slowly. Ask what they already know. Look in the exercise book for a similar example. Resist giving the answer. If you do the work, the teacher cannot see what your child needs help with.

Use what you have

A textbook, a notebook and your child's own class notes are enough. Everyday life offers practice too: counting change at the market, measuring cloth, reading signs and labels, telling stories in your home language.

Praise effort and strategies

Say "You kept going even though that was difficult" or "That was a clever way to check your answer." This builds a child who tries again, which matters more than getting every answer right.

Talk to the teacher

If homework regularly takes much longer than it should, or your child cannot do it alone, tell the teacher. A short note or a visit at the end of the day is enough. Teachers want to know. Ask what your child should be able to do and how you can help at home.

When you cannot be there

  • Ask an older sibling or a neighbour to sit with the child for a short time.
  • Check the exercise book at the weekend and ask your child to show you one thing.
  • Set a simple rule: homework is finished before play, and you will look at it when you are home.

Look after sleep and food

A tired or hungry child cannot concentrate. A regular bedtime and a meal before study are some of the most useful kinds of help you can give.

In short

A steady routine, a few good questions and encouragement matter more than knowing the answers. Talk to the teacher when problems continue.
