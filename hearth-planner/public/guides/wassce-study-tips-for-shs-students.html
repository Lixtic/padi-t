WASSCE Study Tips for SHS Students: Start in Form 1, Not in Form 3

The West African Senior School Certificate Examination (WASSCE), set by the West African Examinations Council (WAEC), is taken at the end of Senior High School. It covers a lot of material from three years. Students who do well usually do not cram. They build good habits early and use past questions wisely. Here is a practical guide.

Know what you will be examined on

Get the list of subjects and the syllabus for each one from your school. The core subjects are taken by everyone, and the electives depend on your programme. Check the topics in the syllabus against your notes, so you know what is covered.

Take good notes from Form 1

Keep a separate, tidy notebook for each subject. After each lesson, spend ten minutes adding a short summary in your own words. These summaries are the best revision material you will ever have.

Use spaced revision

Review a topic the next day, then after a week, then after a month. Short repeated reviews fix material in memory much better than one long session before the exam.

Practise with past questions

  • Work through papers from earlier years, under timed conditions.
  • Mark your answers using the marking scheme or with a teacher.
  • List the topics that keep appearing and the ones where you lose marks.
  • Re-do the questions you got wrong a week later.

Learn the exam technique

  • Read each question carefully and underline the command word, such as state, explain, calculate or discuss.
  • Plan the time for each paper and each section before you begin.
  • In mathematics and science, show your working. Method marks can save you.
  • In English and essay papers, write a brief plan, then write clearly and keep to the point.

Study with others, carefully

A study group can help you explain ideas and catch mistakes. Keep it small, set a topic in advance and stay on task. Avoid sessions that turn into chatting.

Look after yourself

Sleep, food and exercise make a real difference to concentration. A walk or sport after a long study session helps you remember more than another hour of tired reading. Talk to someone you trust if the pressure feels too heavy.

Avoid malpractice

Paper leaks and cheating schemes put your results and future at risk. Trust your own preparation and ask your teachers for help with weak topics.

In short

Keep summaries from Form 1, revise in repeated short sessions, practise with timed past questions, and learn to read questions carefully.
