Scheme of Work in Ghana: What It Is and How to Prepare One

Many Ghanaian teachers use "scheme of work" for the week-by-week list of what they will teach in a term. NaCCA's own curriculum documents call the official version a scheme of learning. The two ideas are close, and the practical question is the same: what do I teach in Week 1, Week 2 and every week after, and how do I keep it on track? This guide explains the terms and shows a simple way to prepare your scheme of work.

Make a termly scheme of learning

Pick a class, subject and term. You get the full week-by-week scheme from the national curriculum, ready to print or open in Word.

Scheme of work, scheme of learning and lesson plan

The three are different sizes of the same plan. It helps to see them as a map, a route and a single stop.

  • Scheme of learning: the term's week-by-week breakdown that goes with the curriculum, listing strands, sub-strands and indicators.
  • Scheme of work: your own term plan. It usually starts from the scheme of learning and adds your school calendar, your class and your resources.
  • Lesson plan: the detailed plan for one lesson, with objective, activities and a check of learning.

Start from the curriculum, not from the textbook

Open the curriculum or scheme of learning for your class and subject first. Copy the strands, sub-strands and indicators in the order they are given. Then look at the textbook to find pages and examples that fit. Starting from the textbook can leave gaps, because a book may skip an indicator or give a topic more space than the curriculum does.

Count your real weeks

A term on paper is not the same as a term in school. Remove the weeks lost to the opening and closing days, mid-term breaks, school events and examinations. Many teachers find they have a few weeks fewer than the scheme assumes. Decide which topics can be shortened and where you will need to catch up.

The columns to include

A clear scheme of work fits on one or two pages. A table with these columns works for most schools.

  • Week and dates.
  • Strand and sub-strand.
  • Content standard and learning indicator.
  • Main teaching and learning activities.
  • Teaching and learning materials.
  • Assessment, for example a class exercise, quiz or project.
  • Remarks, filled in after the week.

Use it every week

A scheme of work only helps if you look at it. On Friday or Sunday, open the next week, turn each indicator into one or two lesson plans, and gather the materials. In the Remarks column, note what you did not finish. That note makes next week's plan honest, and it shows your head teacher or circuit supervisor that you are tracking progress.

In short

Build your scheme of work from the curriculum, count the real weeks, keep it to one table, and use it each week to plan lessons. Write remarks as you go so the plan stays true.
