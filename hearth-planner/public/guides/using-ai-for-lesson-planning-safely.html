Using AI for Lesson Planning: How to Get Help Without Losing Your Judgment

AI tools can draft a lesson plan in a minute. That can save time, but a draft is not a finished plan. AI systems sometimes make mistakes, invent facts or ignore the details of your class. This guide shows how to get the benefit while staying in charge.

What AI is good at

  • Producing a first structure: objectives, phases, activities and resources.
  • Suggesting examples, questions and exercises you can choose from.
  • Reformatting content, for example as a table or a handout.
  • Offering a simpler or harder version of an activity.

Where it can go wrong

  • Stating facts that are not true, or that are out of date.
  • Using examples that do not fit your community.
  • Ignoring your actual scheme of learning or your learners' level.
  • Suggesting activities that need materials you do not have.
  • Mixing up spellings, dates or numbers.

A review checklist

  • Does the objective match the indicator in my scheme of learning?
  • Is every fact correct? Check against the textbook or syllabus.
  • Are the examples and names right for my learners?
  • Can I actually do each activity with the time, space and materials I have?
  • Do learners do something, not only listen?
  • Does the closing task test the objective?
  • Are the questions at a suitable level, and are answers in the marking guide correct?

Give the tool good information

The more specific you are about class, subject, week and learners, the better the draft. Ask for changes in plain language: "make the starter shorter," "use a market example," "add a simpler task for learners who struggle."

Keep the teacher in charge

You know your class and your school. Treat the draft as a colleague's suggestion. Change what does not fit, add your own touches, and sign off on what you will actually teach.

Be careful with learners' data

Do not type learners' full names, photos or private details into any tool. Keep to class-level information. Read a tool's privacy page so that you know how it handles data.

Be honest with learners and colleagues

It is fine to use a drafting tool, as you would use a textbook or a template. What matters is the quality of teaching and that the plan is yours once you have reviewed it.

In short

Use AI to get a quick first draft, then review it against your scheme, your facts and your class. The responsibility for the lesson stays with the teacher.
