Making Low-Cost Teaching and Learning Materials From What You Have

Teaching and learning materials, often called TLMs, make abstract ideas concrete. You do not need to buy anything expensive. Bottle tops, cardboard, seeds, sand, old calendars and pictures from magazines go a long way. Here are ideas by subject, and tips for making materials that last.

Mathematics

  • Bottle tops, stones or seeds for counting, grouping and sharing.
  • A number line painted on a strip of cloth or drawn on the floor with chalk.
  • Place-value cards made from folded card that show hundreds, tens and ones.
  • Fraction strips cut from paper and folded into halves, quarters and eighths.
  • Measuring tasks with string, a ruler made on cardboard, and containers for capacity.

Language and literacy

  • Letter cards and word cards on card stock, kept in a labelled tin.
  • A class word wall with new words and pictures.
  • Story pictures cut from old calendars to sequence and retell.
  • A "sound box" with small objects that begin with a given sound.

Science

  • A plastic bottle cut to make a funnel or a simple rain gauge.
  • Soil, sand and gravel samples in clear jars for observation.
  • Seeds grown in wet cloth to show germination.
  • Leaves and flowers pressed in old notebooks for classification.

Social studies and history

  • A map of the school and community drawn by learners.
  • Photographs, newspaper cuttings and objects from the community.
  • A time line on a long strip of paper with events added by learners.

Make them with learners

When learners help make materials, they learn from the making and take better care of them. A group task such as designing fraction cards is a lesson in itself.

Make them last

  • Cover paper materials with clear tape or plastic if you can.
  • Store them in labelled boxes, so they can be found next year.
  • Ask the school to keep a shared shelf, so that teachers can swap.

Use materials with purpose

A material is useful only if learners handle it and think with it. Plan what learners will do and say with it, and write it in the lesson plan next to the resources line.

In short

Everyday objects can teach number, language and science. Involve learners in making materials, store them well, and always plan how learners will use them.
