Understanding the GES Scheme of Service: A Teacher’s Guide

The Ghana Education Service (GES) Scheme of Service is a workforce and career-management reference. It explains the Service’s structure, grade composition, role expectations, qualifications, competencies, training needs and progression routes. This guide turns the parts most useful to teachers into a practical orientation. It is based on the 2025 GES Scheme of Service supplied for this guide; it is not an individual promotion decision or a substitute for current official instructions.

Why the Scheme was developed

The foreword explains that the Scheme was developed as part of GES institutional reform and the Education Strategic Plan 2018–2030. Its stated purposes include supporting recruitment, career development, job progression, succession planning and human-resource development. It also aims to clarify the managerial, behavioural and technical skills expected across roles (printed pages 12–13).

How the document is organised

  • The opening chapter introduces GES, its object, vision, mission, critical behaviours, functions and organisational structure (printed pages 13–16).
  • The grade-composition section groups positions into professional and sub-professional classes and lists grades across GES divisions and units (printed pages 18–26).
  • Part I covers grade progression and contains detailed grade profiles for roles across the Service.
  • Part II covers positional appointments for the teaching class, including the relationship between grades and school, district, regional and headquarters positions (printed pages 533–536).

GES purpose, behaviours and responsibilities

The document describes the object of the Education Service as coordinating and harmonising the effective and efficient delivery of basic and senior high education. Its stated mission is inclusive and equitable quality formal education and training through effective resource management (printed page 14).

The critical behaviours listed are teamwork, proactiveness, leading by example, time consciousness, creativity and innovation, ownership and accountability, and diligence, hard work and commitment (printed pages 14–15).

Understand the two parts of the Scheme

Part I describes grades and their profiles: job purpose, duties, reporting relationships, qualifications and experience, in-service entry, career progression, skills and competencies, and training and capacity development.

Part II describes positional appointments for the teaching class. It maps grades to positions such as headteacher, assistant headteacher, school improvement and support officer, head of department, district or regional leadership, and schedule officer/teacher. A grade and a position are related, but they are not interchangeable: read the specific appointment rules as well as the grade profile.

Read a grade profile from top to bottom

  • Confirm the exact grade and the division or unit named in the profile.
  • Read “reports to” and “supervises” to understand the role’s place in the structure.
  • Study the job purpose and duties; these describe the work expected at that grade.
  • Check direct-entry and in-service-entry requirements separately.
  • Read the career-progression section for the next grade and its stated conditions.
  • Review the skills, competencies and training areas to identify professional-development priorities.

Important caution about implementation

The implementation-date field in the supplied copy is blank (printed page 17). The document therefore does not establish its own effective date in the copy reviewed here. Confirm with GES or the appropriate HR authority whether the Scheme has been formally brought into operation and which circulars or transitional instructions apply before relying on it for an employment decision.

In short

Use the Scheme as a structured reference: identify the relevant grade and position, read the complete profile, note the evidence and competencies required, and verify current implementation and procedures with GES HR.
