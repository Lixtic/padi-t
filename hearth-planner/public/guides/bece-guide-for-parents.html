The BECE for Parents: What It Is and How to Support Your Child

The Basic Education Certificate Examination, known as the BECE, is taken at the end of Junior High School. It is set and marked by the West African Examinations Council (WAEC). Results are used when placing students into Senior High School, so it is an important moment for families. This guide explains the basics and what you can do. Always check the latest details with the school and with WAEC, because formats and subject lists can change.

What the exam covers

Students sit papers in core subjects such as English Language, Mathematics, Integrated Science and Social Studies, together with other subjects set in the national curriculum for JHS. Papers usually include objective questions and written questions. Ask the school for the list and format for your child's year.

How results are used

Results are reported as grades, and a combined score from selected subjects is used in the school placement process. Because the details can be updated, check the current rules with the school or the official education authorities rather than relying on old information.

Start early, not in the last month

Steady revision across JHS 3 works much better than last-minute cramming. Encourage your child to review each topic soon after it is taught, then again some weeks later. This spacing helps memory.

Help with a revision plan

  • List the subjects and the topics your child finds hardest.
  • Give the hardest topics more time, and shorter, more frequent sessions.
  • Include short breaks and at least one day of rest each week.
  • Use past questions, which the school can often supply, to practise the format.

Practise under exam conditions

Once a month, let your child answer a past paper in a set time without notes. Then go through the answers together, and ask them to explain the ones they got wrong. Understanding the mistake matters more than the mark.

Look after health and confidence

  • Regular meals, water and enough sleep, especially the week before the exam.
  • Calm words. Avoid comparing your child with others.
  • A plan for exam day: travel, materials, and a calm morning.

Stay in touch with the school

Ask the teachers where your child needs most help. Go to any mock exams and parent meetings. If you can afford extra lessons, choose them carefully, and check that your child is not too tired to study at home.

Beware of exam malpractice

Cheating and leaked paper schemes can lead to cancelled results and serious trouble. Tell your child clearly that honest work is the only safe way, and avoid anyone who offers "sure" questions for payment.

In short

Start early, plan revision with your child, practise with past questions, protect rest and health, and stay in touch with the school.
