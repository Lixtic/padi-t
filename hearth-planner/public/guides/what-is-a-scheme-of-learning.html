What Is a Scheme of Learning, and How Do You Use It?

Teachers in Ghana hear the terms scheme of learning, scheme of work, syllabus and lesson plan used almost interchangeably. They are related but not the same, and knowing the difference saves time and mistakes. This guide explains what a scheme of learning is and how to use it well.

The big picture: curriculum, scheme, lesson plan

The curriculum sets out what learners are expected to learn in each subject and class. The scheme of learning takes a year of that curriculum and spreads it across the terms and weeks. A lesson plan then takes one week, or one lesson, and says how you will teach it. Think of it as a journey: the curriculum is the destination, the scheme is the route with stops, and the lesson plan is the detailed directions for one leg.

What you usually find in a scheme of learning

  • The term and week number.
  • The strand and sub-strand, which name the topic area.
  • The content standard and the indicators for that stretch of teaching.
  • Sometimes suggested resources or notes on assessment.
  • Weeks set aside for revision, exams or school activities, so you can see real teaching time.

Why not just follow your own order?

It is tempting to teach the topics you like first. The trouble is that learners move between classes and schools, and many ideas build on earlier ones. Following the scheme keeps your class in step with the curriculum and with the exam, and it makes a handover easy if another teacher takes over your class.

How to use it each week

Open the scheme on Sunday evening or at the start of the week. Find the row for the coming week and read the indicators out loud to yourself. Ask what learners must already know to manage them. If a gap shows up, plan a short recap in the starter.

  • Mark any week that your school calendar shortens, such as a holiday, a sports day or an exam week.
  • If you fall behind, merge two closely related indicators rather than dropping a whole topic.
  • Keep a simple tick list so you can see at a glance what has been covered.

When the scheme and the real class do not match

Sometimes learners are not ready for the week on the page. The scheme is a guide, not a command. It is reasonable to spend an extra day on a hard idea, provided you plan the catch-up and tell your head teacher. What matters is that you can explain why you changed the order.

Using your own scheme

Some schools prepare their own scheme, or a teacher adapts the official one to local needs. That is fine, as long as it still covers the content of the curriculum for the year. GuidEd lets a teacher upload their own scheme file so that weekly plans follow it instead of the built-in one.

In short

The scheme of learning tells you what to teach and when. The lesson plan tells you how. Use the scheme as your calendar, and plan each week from its row.
