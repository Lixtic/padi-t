Planning the Term and Keeping Your Workload Manageable

Teaching includes planning, marking, records, meetings and school duties on top of the lessons themselves. Without a system, the evenings disappear. A few habits at the start of term and a simple weekly rhythm can give some of that time back.

Plan the term on one page

At the start of the term, take your scheme of learning and a school calendar. Mark holidays, exam weeks, sports days and any other events. Count the real teaching weeks. Write the topics against the weeks so you can see the whole term at a glance.

Plan backwards from exams and reports

Note when tests, end-of-term exams and report deadlines fall. Leave a week for revision before them. If time is tight, decide early which topics to compress, rather than rushing at the end.

Set a weekly rhythm

  • One fixed time to plan the coming week, such as Friday afternoon or Sunday evening.
  • One fixed time to catch up on records and marks.
  • A short daily reflection of two lines on what worked.

Reuse and adapt

A good lesson plan from last year can be improved, not rewritten from nothing. Keep plans in a folder by class and topic. Share and swap with colleagues who teach the same class. Tools that draft a plan, such as GuidEd, can save the first hour of work, but you still adjust and check the plan before you teach.

Mark with a purpose

Decide what each piece of marking is for. Some tasks need detailed comments and a mark. Others need a quick tick and a note of common mistakes. Peer and self-marking save time and teach learners to review their own work.

Keep simple records

A single class list with columns for tests, tasks and notes is often enough. Update it the same day. It will make reports and parent meetings much easier.

Protect some time for yourself

Decide a time when you stop work. Use a checklist so that you do not carry every task in your head. A rested teacher teaches better than a tired one.

In short

Plan the term on one page, keep a fixed weekly rhythm, reuse and adapt plans, and match the amount of marking to its purpose.
