Guides for teachers and families

Plain-language help with lesson planning, teaching and learning at home, written for the Ghanaian classroom. Each guide is short, practical and free to read.

For teachers

  • How to Write a GES Lesson Plan, Step by Step
    A practical walkthrough of the parts of a lesson plan used in Ghanaian schools, with tips for writing each part so the lesson runs smoothly.
  • What Is a Scheme of Learning, and How Do You Use It?
    Understand how a scheme of learning differs from a lesson plan, what it contains, and how to turn each week of it into real teaching.
  • The Core Competencies in the Ghanaian Curriculum, Explained Simply
    What the core competencies are, why they appear in lesson plans, and easy classroom examples for each one.
  • Writing Learning Indicators and Objectives That You Can Actually Measure
    How to turn a curriculum indicator into a clear, testable lesson objective, with before-and-after examples.
  • Starter, Main Instruction and Closure: Running the Three Phases of a Lesson
    Practical ideas and timing for the three phases of a lesson, with examples that work in large or low-resource classrooms.
  • Assessment as, for and of Learning: What Each Means in Your Classroom
    A clear explanation of the three purposes of assessment and simple ways to use each in everyday teaching.
  • Better Questions: Moving Learners From Recall to Thinking
    How to ask questions at different levels of thinking, with examples for primary and JHS classes, so that lessons go beyond memorising.
  • Teaching a Large Class: Practical Strategies That Work With Limited Resources
    Realistic ideas for managing, engaging and assessing 50 or more learners with few textbooks and little space.
  • Making Low-Cost Teaching and Learning Materials From What You Have
    Ideas for making TLMs from local and recycled items that help learners understand number, language, science and social studies.
  • Teaching Early Reading in Lower Primary: Sounds, Words and Stories
    Simple, practical steps for helping Basic 1 to 3 learners become confident readers in English and a Ghanaian language.
  • Classroom Management for New Teachers: Calm, Clear and Consistent
    Simple habits for starting your first classes well, handling noise and misbehaviour, and building respect without shouting.
  • Planning the Term and Keeping Your Workload Manageable
    A realistic approach to term planning, weekly preparation and marking so that you stay organised without burning out.
  • Using AI for Lesson Planning: How to Get Help Without Losing Your Judgment
    What AI tools can and cannot do for teachers, and a checklist for reviewing any AI-written lesson plan before you teach it.

For parents and students

  • Helping Your Child With Homework When You Are Busy or Did Not Go Far in School
    Practical ways for Ghanaian parents to support homework, even with little time or little schooling of their own.
  • The BECE for Parents: What It Is and How to Support Your Child
    A clear overview of the Basic Education Certificate Examination and practical ways parents can support a JHS 3 child through it.
  • A Simple BECE Revision Timetable for JHS 3 Students
    How to build a realistic revision timetable for the BECE, with a sample weekly layout and tips on what to study when.
  • WASSCE Study Tips for SHS Students: Start in Form 1, Not in Form 3
    Study habits, past-question practice and exam techniques for SHS students preparing for the WASSCE.
  • Building a Study Routine at Home That Children Will Actually Follow
    How to set up a realistic daily study habit for primary and JHS children, including time, place, breaks and motivation.
  • Helping a Child Who Struggles With Mathematics
    Why maths feels hard for some children and what parents can do at home to rebuild confidence and fill gaps.

GuidEd is an independent tool and is not an official product of the Ghana Education Service, NaCCA or WAEC. Guides are general advice.
